Can a pill change your skin tone?

Skin-brightening supplements come with big promises. Below are six claims you'll often see. Decide whether each one holds up, then see what the research says.

Every shade in the strip above is healthy skin. This page is about skin health, not skin color.

"A supplement can permanently change your natural skin tone."

Your natural skin tone is set largely by genetics: how much melanin your body makes and which type it favors. No oral supplement rewrites that, and the FDA has not approved any supplement for permanently changing skin tone.

"You'll see results from an oral supplement within a week."

Skin renews on a cycle of about 28 days in your twenties, stretching to 40 to 60 days later in life. Studies on oral glutathione typically measure results after 8 to 12 weeks, and the changes they report are modest.

"Glutathione, vitamin C, and collagen all work the same way."

Glutathione acts mainly as an antioxidant. Vitamin C supports collagen formation and helps recycle glutathione. Collagen peptides target hydration and elasticity, not pigment. They're not interchangeable.

"You still need sunscreen if you take a skin supplement."

UV exposure triggers new melanin production, which can create or deepen dark spots. No supplement replaces daily broad-spectrum sunscreen. It's the step that protects every other part of your routine.

"Some ingredients may help soften the look of dark spots over time."

Some compounds may modestly slow tyrosinase, an enzyme involved in pigment production. That can help dark spots look softer over weeks to months. Softening a spot is very different from changing your baseline tone.

"Dietary supplements are FDA-approved before they go on sale."

In the U.S., the FDA does not approve dietary supplements before they're sold. That's why supplement labels carry a disclaimer, and why third-party testing is worth looking for.
